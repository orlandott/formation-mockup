The problem

What is lock-in?

Lock-in is where some technological, political, or institutional feature of the world is held stable for a long time. Possibly for centuries. Possibly forever.

Some lock-ins are harmless. QWERTY survives only because switching costs too much. Others would be catastrophic, like a totalitarian regime or a monopoly over resources and labour held in place indefinitely.

AI is the reason to take this seriously now. Digital systems can correct their own errors and copy themselves without drifting, so an arrangement built on them could hold for centuries or millennia.

Focus areas

The scenarios we study

AI-enabled totalitarianism

A dictator could use AI systems to improve their own lifespan and implement mass worldwide surveillance, leading to a long-term stable totalitarian regime.

Extreme power concentration

The people at the forefront of AI could gain lasting leverage over humanity's direction, with monopolies over resources and labour.

Our approach

What we do about it

We research how lock-in happens, and build practical interventions that reduce the risk.

We build our own models from first principles and work openly with other AI safety researchers, aiming at defences that apply to today's systems.

Current research focus

Secret loyalties

A secret loyalty is a hidden objective built into an AI model. It favours a particular person or group, and is switched on by a private activation condition.

Secret loyalties are a concrete route to AI-enabled authoritarianism, and they can be studied and defended against in today's systems. That is what our technical work focuses on.
