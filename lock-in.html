Explainer

What is lock-in?

Lock-in is where some feature of the world — technological, political, or institutional — is held stable for a long time. Possibly for centuries. Possibly forever.

The mechanics

How lock-in works

It comes from irreversibility

Lock-in is a physical phenomenon. It emerges wherever a change is much easier to make than to undo.

It feeds itself

Once embedded, locked-in elements reinforce themselves and resist change.

It happens at every scale

From the technology choices of a single organisation up to global ideologies.

It can last a very long time

Years, decades, or millennia.

Examples

What lock-in looks like

The QWERTY keyboard

The classic harmless example. QWERTY is the standard keyboard layout not because it is the best design for typing, but because the typewriters that used it became the most popular — and the cost of switching to a better layout is now too high.

Stable totalitarianism

A potentially catastrophic lock-in. Regimes have always ended with their rulers, but AI systems do not age, forget, or die — an AI-enabled leader could hold a state in place for centuries.

AI-driven power concentration

Control over AI systems could give the people at the forefront of the technology enormous leverage over humanity's technological and political direction, creating persistent monopolies over resources and labour.

Ideological stasis

A dogmatic, anti-rational belief system could come to dominate culture, locking in norms and practices that reinforce themselves.

An analogy

The lobster pot

Easy to get in. Hard to get out.

A lock-in works like a lobster pot: entering is the natural, even attractive move, and by the time the problem is clear, the way back is gone. That is why we study lock-in before it happens — afterwards may be too late.

Why it matters

Lock-in risk is serious and neglected

Potentially catastrophic

A negative lock-in could affect a substantial number of people.

Neglected

Few organisations or individuals track or model lock-in risk.

A moral problem

It could perpetuate or create suffering and prevent progress.

A global problem

It could affect institutions, states, nations, or the world.
